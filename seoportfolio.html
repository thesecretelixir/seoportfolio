how I write for search

My SEO approach.

Most writers treat SEO as a checklist: stuff in keywords, hit a word count, add some headers. That is not how sustainable rankings happen. I start with the human first. What is someone actually trying to understand when they type that query? Then I build content that answers that intent more completely than anything else on the page.

SEO is not about tricking Google. It is about being genuinely the best answer to a question. I write to be that answer.

Working at Dr Batra's taught me how to build topical authority in a regulated, sensitive category. Healthcare content has a higher bar. E-E-A-T (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trust) is not optional, it is survival. Every article had to hold up medically, emotionally, and strategically.

At Bold Care, the category demands a different kind of writing. Content here needs to be clear, credible, and genuinely useful to someone with a real question. That pressure shaped how I think about every brief.

01
Intent mapping before keywords
I research what someone actually wants when they search a term: informational, navigational, transactional, or investigational. The keyword is just the surface. Intent determines structure.
02
SERP analysis before writing
I study every result on page one: what format won, what depth won, what angle is missing. I write to fill the gap, not clone the leader.
03
Structure search engines can read
Proper H-tag hierarchy, featured snippet targets, FAQ schema hooks, clean internal linking. Content that is as navigable for a crawler as it is for a person.
04
Writing humans actually finish
Dwell time and engagement signals matter. An article that ranks but bounces is failing. I write in a way that keeps people reading because they want to, not because they have to.
GEO in action

Bold Care ranks in ChatGPT.

I write content that AI models cite, not just content that search engines index.

Bold Care operates in a category that most AI models handle cautiously. Getting ChatGPT to surface your content requires more than keywords. It requires being a credible, structured, citable source. The Bold Care condom guide was written with that in mind: clear product breakdowns, honest comparisons, zero fluff, no brand-first framing.

The result: when users ask ChatGPT about ultra-thin condoms in India, Bold Care's content gets cited. That is GEO. Generative Engine Optimisation. And it is becoming as valuable as a first-page ranking.

Why it works: AI models pull from content that is authoritative, well-structured, and genuinely useful. The Bold Care guide was written to answer every real question a buyer has, not just the keyword. That specificity is what gets cited.
